One observation per line; corpus-wide figures use the 659 human-reviewed pages unless a split is named.

- 2026-09-22 | `semantic-v3` corpus review | On the current 659 human-reviewed labels, author accuracy was 62.7% overall and 62.2% when present; ancestor and descendant boundary mistakes remained common, motivating the following two experiments.
- 2026-09-22 | `model/best.pt` (`semantic-v3`, baseline) | Author exact-node accuracy was 62.7% overall and 62.2% when author labels were present; 76 errors selected an ancestor and 103 selected a descendant.
- 2026-09-22 | Ancestor-margin weight 1.0 | Ancestor errors fell 76→54, but author accuracy fell 62.7%→59.6% and descendant errors rose 103→126; held-out author accuracy stayed 83.3% (30 pages).
- 2026-09-22 | Ancestor-margin weight 0.25 | Ancestor errors remained at 54, descendant errors rose to 127, and author accuracy was 60.7%; reducing the loss weight did not reverse the accuracy drop.
- 2026-09-22 | Ancestor-margin weight 0.05 | Ancestor errors fell only to 67, descendant errors rose to 106, and author accuracy was 61.2%; the ancestor penalty alone did not improve extraction.
- 2026-09-22 | Ancestor-margin sweep | Keep the experiment opt-in and leave the production checkpoint unchanged; the loss moves predictions from broad parents toward descendants or missing, rather than improving the exact boundary overall.
- 2026-09-22 | Two-stage authors, unweighted presence loss | Validation presence accuracy reached 98%, but the model predicted authors present on all 659 pages; 71 absent-author pages became false positives and corpus-wide author accuracy was 51.6%.
- 2026-09-22 | Two-stage authors, training-split class weights (negative 3.30, positive 0.589) | At the selected checkpoint, validation absent-author recall was still 0%; high validation presence accuracy reflected the split's 98% positive rate.
- 2026-09-22 | Two-stage authors, class-balanced checkpoint | Full-corpus author accuracy was 48.9% overall and 46.4% on present-author pages; predictions included 93 missing-author decisions and 22 false-present decisions.
- 2026-09-22 | Two-stage authors, all fields | Full-corpus article, date, and summary accuracy fell to 79.1%, 91.4%, and 63.1%; title remained 95.8%, showing that the shared encoder's retraining also changed non-author predictions.
- 2026-09-22 | Two-stage authors, held-out test | Author accuracy stayed 83.3% on 30 pages, but this split has no absent-author examples and does not establish that the presence gate generalizes.
- 2026-09-22 | Two-stage authors | Do not promote this checkpoint; page-level presence is learned, but present-only node selection and split imbalance remain unresolved.
- 2026-09-22 | Local author-boundary hypothesis | The existing `model/best.pt` selected an ancestor on 76 pages and a descendant on 103 pages. Keep that checkpoint frozen and compare its predicted author node with nearby parents and children, so this experiment targets wrapper boundaries without changing other fields or turning a `missing` prediction into a present author.
- 2026-09-22 | Local candidate design | Search the predicted node, up to five ancestors, and descendants up to seven levels deep (capped at 192 candidates). The ranker uses the base author score, relative word coverage and text length, author/profile links, DOM shape, and byline/date/reading-time markers; the target is the human-labeled exact node.

- 2026-09-22 | Local ranker model shape | This is a separate 24-hidden-unit, one-layer neural ranker on top of the frozen base extractor, not a new text encoder. For each nearby node it scores 76 numbers: 49 base numeric features plus 27 local comparison features. The local features include whether a node is the seed, parent, or child; relative base-model author score; word coverage and text length versus the seed; links and name-list shape; date and reading-time markers; and DOM tag/child counts. Semantic embeddings are not passed directly to the ranker, but influence it indirectly through the base author score.
- 2026-09-22 | Local ranker training details | The loss compares the human-labeled author node with the other local nodes only when that gold node is reachable; 331 train and 185 validation pages meet that condition. Use train data to fit weights, validation loss to select epoch 25, validation exact-node accuracy to select margin 0.0, and the 30-page held-out site only for a final check. A base `missing` prediction is never changed by this model.
- 2026-09-22 | Local ranker accuracy detail | Train exact-node author accuracy improved 284/429→307/429 (+23 net); validation improved 104/200→136/200 (+32 net); test stayed 25/30. Across the entire corpus it improved 413/659→468/659 (+55 net, or 62.7%→71.0%). On pages with a present author label, it improved 366/588→421/588 (62.2%→71.6%). The all-corpus and validation figures are useful for iteration but are not independent evidence of generalization.
- 2026-09-22 | Local ranker error analysis | Validation had 33 formerly wrong pages fixed and one formerly correct page broken; train had 42 fixes and 19 regressions. OpenAI Developers supplied 25 of 32 net validation fixes, and the held-out site had no gain. On the LinkedIn “Reimagining LinkedIn’s search tech stack” upload, both stages still chose a parent containing the author and date over the author-only child, even though the child was in the local search; the ranker scored them 3.03 versus 1.34. This remains a known boundary error, not a solved example.
- 2026-09-22 | Default-model decision | Bundle the exact 29 KB `semantic-v3` base checkpoint and matching 11 KB boundary refiner in the Python package and load both when no checkpoint is specified. Keep explicit custom checkpoints base-only unless the caller also supplies a matching refiner, preventing accidental reuse of weights tied to another checkpoint SHA-256. The default improves observed fit but does not imply the held-out test improved; continue evaluating new sites before replacing any training assumptions.
- 2026-09-22 | Primary-author policy | Treat `authors` as the primary byline author or authors. Ignore a separate “Co-authors” list and later contributor biographies. For multiple primary byline names, select the tightest single wrapper that includes all primary names; this policy still needs a consistent audit of existing annotations.
